Current carrying capacity of cables

The current carrying capacity of a cable, also known as ampacity, is the maximum amount of electric current a cable can safely carry without exceeding its temperature rating. This capacity depends on several factors including the cable's material, insulation type, installation conditions, and ambient temperature.

There’s no single “current vs cross-section” curve independent of conditions. However, here is a table with typical values for copper cables:

Suitable Cable Cross-Sectional Area (mm²)Typical Current Rating (A)Circuit Breaker Rating (A)
1.5 mm²7.9 – 15.9 A8 A
2.5 mm²15.9 – 22 A15 A
4 mm²22 – 30 A20 A
6 mm²30 – 39 A30 A
10 mm²39 – 54 A40 A
16 mm²54 – 72 A60 A

50 mm²117 – 147 A125 A
70 mm²147 – 180 A150 A

More precise values can be obtained from cable manufacturers' datasheets or electrical standards such as the National Electrical Code (NEC) or IEC standards (IEC 60364-5-52), which take into account specific installation conditions and safety factors.
